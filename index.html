About

I’m a second-year PhD student in Operations, Technology, and Information Management at the Cornell SC Johnson College of Business. I study how people interact and are organized in firms and society.

Methodologically, I use both econometric analysis and economic modeling to understand business and social problems.

Before joining Cornell, I obtained my Bachelor’s degree in Management Science from the School of Management at Fudan University, with an additional year of major coursework in Physics.

Research

I am broadly interested in how technology and organizational structure shape human behavior and outcomes, in both business and social settings.

Work in Progress

Agentic AI, Organization, and Work Design

I study how AI that can act autonomously (“agentic AI”) changes the way work is divided, supervised, and coordinated inside organizations. The goal is to understand when AI substitutes for human effort, when it complements human judgment, and how these shifts reshape organizational structure and incentives.

Breaking Enrollment Barriers for Refugee Children: Evidence from an Enrollment Support Program in Turkey

This project combines rich administrative and program data from a large NGO to study which children are most at risk of being out of school and how targeted support helps them enroll and stay in education. I focus on operational rules, targeting, and heterogeneous treatment effects.

  • Incoming presentation at POMS 2026
  • Presented at INFORMS 2025

China’s Technology Partnerships: How Export Controls Reshape Global Collaboration

I examine how U.S. export controls reshape global technology collaboration involving Chinese firms and inventors. Using patent and collaboration data, I study how restrictions propagate through international R&D networks and how firms adapt their partnerships.

  • Best Graduation Thesis Award, School of Management, Fudan University
