Hobbies & Personal Projects

Outside of formal research, I like using code, tools, and small experiments to make everyday life with friends (and with myself) a bit more interesting and intentional.

Obsidian & Personal Knowledge

I am a deep user of Obsidian. I maintain a detailed research log there and track how I spend my time across tasks and projects. I use scripts and small pieces of code to summarize my daily work and generate statistics automatically.

Over time, this has grown into a personal knowledge system: I can see how ideas evolve, how different projects connect, and where my attention actually goes. I’m exploring ways to turn these notes into visual knowledge maps and other tools that make my thinking more transparent to myself.

In the future, I would like to experiment with building my own Obsidian plugin to support research logging and time-aware knowledge graphs.

Small Web Experiments with Friends

I enjoy building small websites to make time with friends more fun and memorable. Some examples include:

  • Truth or Dare
    Party tool

  • 100 Things That Could Warm Your Winter
    Joy project

Why This Matters to Me

At a high level, my research and my hobbies are connected by the same question: how can we design systems that make it easier for people to do meaningful work and enjoy their lives?

Sometimes the answer is a formal model or an econometric study. Sometimes it is a tiny web app, a better note-taking workflow, or a game with friends.
